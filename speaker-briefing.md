# Working with Me as Your Speaker

Thank you for inviting me — I'm genuinely looking forward to this. What follows are the questions I ask before every event, much like a doctor asks a few questions before treatment: not bureaucracy, but the fastest way to make sure you get exactly the contribution your event needs. Get these sorted early and we can both focus on the good part — a great session for your audience, and ideally some fun along the way.

## Before the event

**1. The objective of your event**
What do you want the event to achieve? Knowing your goal helps me contribute to it rather than beside it.

**2. My role in the bigger picture**
How does my contribution support that objective? When I understand where I fit, I can connect my talk to the sessions around it and reinforce your message instead of just delivering mine.

**3. Audience size**
How many people will be in the room? A talk for 15 people is a different talk than one for 300 — I'll prepare the right one for you.

**4. Audience composition**
Who will be there — roles, seniority, technical background? I tailor depth and examples to the people in front of me, so the more you tell me, the better it lands for them.

**5. Language**
German, English or French — I'm happy to present in any of the three. Tell me which one fits your audience best, and also whether I'll be talking to native speakers or not: for an audience listening in their second language, I slow down, drop the idioms, and keep the wording plainer — it makes a real difference to how much sticks.

**6. Date and my exact timeslot**
The event date and my precise slot — start time, duration, whether Q&A is included — let me plan travel and pace my content so it fits your program exactly.

**7. A calendar entry, maintained by you — with location and access included**
Then put exactly that slot into my calendar: an Outlook invitation, kept up to date, works wonders. My calendar runs my life, so if the invite is current, I'll be exactly where you need me, when you need me. It helps a lot if the invite itself contains everything I need to find you: venue address, how I get in (badge, access code, parking or public transport), and — once inside — either a contact person who meets me or a description of where to go (building, floor, room). Standing in a lobby not knowing where to turn is a silly way for both of us to start the day.

**8. Registration**
If you can register me for the event yourself, that's one thing off both our plates — a short confirmation that I'm on the list is all I need.

**9. Am I a guest or a touch-and-go speaker?**
Please tell me whether you'd like me to stick around — am I invited to the event itself, or just to my slot? This is a purely factual question, no judgement either way: both are fine, I just plan my day (and my conversations) differently.

**10. Slide templates and handouts**
A small heads-up so you're not surprised: I can't use your event template. When I speak for my employer, I present in my employer's branded slides; otherwise I use my own — my slides need to match who I represent on stage.

It also helps me if you're precise about the materials you need. Handouts, for example, are something I prepare separately — they won't be the slides I show, but a document with additional explanation (except my employer's slides, which I can share as shown). Tell me early what you need and you'll get it.

**11. Promotion — happy to help**
If you'd like me to promote the event on my own channels, just send me ready-to-use social media images and I'll gladly share them.

**12. Prep calls — short and focused**
I'm glad to jump on a prep call to align on content, and a check-in shortly before the conference. 25 minutes each is usually plenty — you can skip the standard speaker briefing with me and spend the time on what really matters: your content and your audience. And if the content itself needs real joint preparation, we'll simply take that time together.

## During the event

**13. Timing and stage setup**
When I present, I'm fully with the audience — and I'm very precise on time, as long as I have orientation. So please give me a timer or clock in my line of sight, big enough to read at a glance; with that, I'll land my slot spot on and hand over to the next item on time. Also let me know which rule applies: a fixed duration (if I start late, I end late) or a hard stop at a set time — I'm happy with either, I just need to know which one. And if a confidence monitor with my slides is possible, it helps me stay facing the room instead of my own projection.

**14. Media — you have my approval**
You have my consent to create photos, video, and audio of my contribution during the event and to publish them internally and externally in relation to it — no separate release form needed.

## After the event

**15. Share the media with me**
Please share the photos and recordings of me with me, with your approval to use them for my own purposes and channels. A fair exchange for point 14 — and your event gets extra reach afterwards.

**16. What worked for you — and what you would have wished for**
My goal is simple: I'd love for you to invite me again. So after the event, two questions matter to me: What did you and your audience appreciate about my contribution? And what would you have wished for — more of, less of, different? Framed that way, it's easy to answer and gives me exactly what I need to serve your objectives even better next time. Happy to do this as a 25-minute post-event call, too.
